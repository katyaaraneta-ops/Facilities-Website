# Facilities, Incorporated — Brand Voice Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0 (generated April 2026 from website discovery)
**Primary audience:** Office space seekers — brokers, agents, and company owners looking for space in Mandaluyong / Metro Manila

## 1. Brand Foundation

### Who We Are
Facilities, Incorporated is a family-owned Philippine real estate developer founded in 1960. We've grown from insurance and trading into one of Metro Manila's most established property developers — with over six decades of landmark projects along Shaw Boulevard, Mandaluyong City.

### What We Do
We develop, lease, and manage commercial and office properties in Metro Manila. Our flagship assets are **Summit One Tower** and **Facilities Centre**, both located along Shaw Boulevard. We are currently expanding through joint ventures, new commercial centers, and residential communities nationwide.

### Core Tagline
> *"Quality has no substitute."*

Use this sparingly — it's a closing statement, not a headline. It lands best when it follows a description of what we've built or how we operate.

## 2. Audience

### Primary Audience
**Office space seekers in Metro Manila** — specifically:
- **Brokers and agents** sourcing space for clients
- **Company owners and decision-makers** looking to lease office space (SMEs, corporates, BPOs, tech companies)
- **PEZA-eligible businesses** seeking accredited commercial space

### What they care about
- Floor area and layout flexibility
- Monthly cost and association dues
- Location relative to MRT, expressways, and key business districts
- Building quality, backup power, connectivity
- Who to call and how fast they can move in

### What they don't need
Emotional storytelling or corporate history as a primary pitch. Lead with the space. Bring in the heritage when it builds trust, not as the opener.

## 3. Voice Attributes

### 3.1 Authoritative, not boastful
Back claims with specifics. "46-storey" beats "towering." "PEZA-accredited" beats "world-class." "First development in the Philippines to feature underground utility wiring" beats "innovative."

✅ **Do:** "Summit One Tower is the tallest building on Shaw Boulevard, with full fiber-optic connectivity and 100% backup power."
❌ **Don't:** "An amazing, state-of-the-art tower in the heart of Mandaluyong!"

### 3.2 Measured and restrained
Avoid hype language. No exclamation points in body copy. One claim at a time, stated plainly.

✅ **Do:** "Facilities Centre offers the longest street frontage along Shaw Boulevard, with anchor banking tenants and easy MRT access."
❌ **Don't:** "Don't miss this incredible opportunity to set up your business in an unbeatable location!"

### 3.3 Legacy-conscious, but not stuck in the past
Our 60+ year history is a trust signal — use it to reassure, not to reminisce. Pair it with forward momentum.

✅ **Do:** "Founded in 1960, Facilities Inc. brings six decades of property expertise to every leasing relationship."
❌ **Don't:** "Back in 1960, our founders had a vision..." (navel-gazing)

### 3.4 Precise and detail-forward
Give the numbers. Tenants and brokers make decisions on specifics. Floor area, price, availability date, floor level — always include these when relevant.

### 3.5 Professionally warm
We are formal but not cold. Mercy runs leasing operations — she's the human face of Facilities Inc. Calls-to-action should feel accessible ("Make an inquiry," "Call Mercy") without being casual or pushy.

## 4. Tone by Content Type

| Content Type | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit listings** | Factual, precise, confident | Specs first, then a 2–3 sentence description of the space feel |
| **Project pages** | Legacy-meets-modern, proud | Lead with the landmark status, then practical details |
| **Blog / SEO articles** | Helpful expert, neutral | Educate the reader first; only mention Facilities Inc. properties where genuinely relevant |
| **Contact / inquiry prompts** | Warm, direct, low-pressure | "Our leasing team is ready to schedule a viewing." |
| **Vision / investor content** | Strategic, forward-looking | Reserved for investor/JV audiences; separate tone from leasing copy |

## 5. Preferred Terminology

### Use These
| Preferred | Instead Of |
|---|---|
| landmark property / landmark asset | building, development |
| premier commercial hub | top office building |
| operational footprint | portfolio (when speaking of managed properties) |
| management standards | property management services |
| anchor tenants | big tenants |
| strategically located | conveniently near |
| floor area | office size |
| association dues | condo fees / HOA |
| PEZA-accredited | PEZA-registered |
| fitted | furnished (when describing condition) |

### Avoid
- Startup/tech jargon: "ecosystem," "seamless," "solution," "synergy"
- Aggressive sales language: "best deal," "hurry," "limited slots only"
- Vague superlatives without backing: "world-class," "top-notch," "amazing"
- Filler words: "very," "really," "truly"

## 6. SEO Writing Guidelines

> The blog exists primarily to drive Google traffic from people searching for office space in Mandaluyong and Metro Manila. Voice in blog posts should be **helpful and informational first** — we are the expert guide, not the salesperson.

### Blog Voice Rules
1. **Lead with what the reader needs to know**, not with who we are
2. Use the exact keyword phrase in the H1, first paragraph, and at least 2–3 subheadings
3. Write in second person ("you," "your team") for blog posts — it's more engaging and conversational
4. Only mention Facilities Inc. properties organically, when genuinely relevant to the topic
5. Keep paragraphs to 2–4 sentences. Break up with numbered or bulleted lists where it aids scannability
6. Aim for 800–1,500 words per article (enough for Google to treat it as substantive)
7. End every post with a clear CTA linking to the relevant unit listing or contact page

### Blog Tone vs. Main Site Tone
This dual-mode approach is intentional:
- **Main site** = brand voice (heritage, prestige, precision)
- **Blog** = editorial/expert voice (practical, informational, reader-first)

Both are Facilities Inc., just speaking to the reader at different stages of their decision journey.

---

## 7. What We Don't Say

- We don't lead with price (it comes after the value is established)
- We don't use Gmail-style informality in marketing copy (even if our leasing contact uses a personal email)
- We don't make claims we can't verify with specifics
- We don't use the word "luxury" — our positioning is *quality* and *prestige*, which are earned through evidence, not declared
